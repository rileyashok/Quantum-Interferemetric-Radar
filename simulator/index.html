    How it works

    Setup

    An emitter at altitude h₀ sends three beams down to detectors at a ground station. Beams A and B are vertical and D apart. The cone beam is slanted: it starts w₀ to the left of beam A at the emitter and converges onto the same ground point as beam A. A plane flying straight across cuts the cone first, then A, then B.

    From photons to edges

    Each detector receives photons at random times, at a steady average rate. While the plane is in the way, only dark counts arrive. The simulator first counts photons in short time bins to find roughly when each beam went dark. It then fits the exact moment from the individual photon arrival times near each edge (a maximum-likelihood fit).

    Detected rate with the beam open = flux × sky transmission × detector efficiency + dark counts. Timing jitter blurs every timestamp.

    Formulas (same as the team's SPI-R Backend Calculations)

    • tᵣ = delay between beam A and beam B being cut → speed v = D / tᵣ
    • tₛ = time A and B are both blocked (negative means a gap: the plane is shorter than D) → length L = D (tₛ / tᵣ + 1)
    • Δt = delay between the cone and beam A being cut → d₁ = v Δt. By similar triangles d₁ / h = w₀ / h₀, so altitude h = h₀ d₁ / w₀

    tᵣ and Δt are averaged over the nose and tail edges to cut noise.

    Checks

    Named after the placeholder functions in the team's backend. Their thresholds were left blank, so these are our picks: sky transmission below 5%, dark counts above 10% of the signal, fewer than about 120 photons per blocked beam, and estimated speed below 35 m/s (not applied to the tabletop model).

    Not modeled yet

    • Interference or any quantum effects (the "interferometric" part of the project name).
    • Beam width, divergence, pointing error and atmospheric turbulence: beams are ideal thin lines.
    • The plane's height and wings: it is a line along its flight path that fully blocks a beam.
    • Planes not flying straight across the beams, a moving satellite, and Earth's curvature.
    • Background sunlight and stray reflections (fold them into dark counts for now).
